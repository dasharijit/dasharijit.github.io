I am a PhD Scholar at the University of Cambridge, researching in the domain of Development Economics. I study how transport infrastructure shapes regional economic trajectories in India, with a focus on spatial inequality and on the conditions under which public investment spreads growth more fairly. Specifically my research aims to unpack the link between transport infrastructure and regional economic growth in the backdrop of Indian States by investigating not simply whether Transport Infrastructure Investments raises growth. Rather my research attempts to highlight which institutional, land-use and political conditions determine whether Transport infrastructure investment raises economic growth more equitably. My supervisor is Professor Ozge Oner.

Before commencing my Doctoral research at Cambridge, I worked as an Economic Analyst at the Social Investment Business Group in London, where I led the Economic Impact assessment of the £142 million Futurebuilders England fund. Earlier, I was involved in Economic Policy research at NITI Aayog and the Indian Institute of Public Administration in New Delhi.

I am affiliated with Research Impact Lab, where I work as a consultant on evidence and impact evaluation for public policy.

Doctoral research · supervised by Professor Ozge Oner

Summary

My doctoral work asks a question beyond whether transport infrastructure investment raises growth. I examine which institutional, land-use and political conditions determine whether that investment raises growth more equitably across Indian states and regions.

Research interests: spatial inequality, infrastructure, regional economic development, urban economics.
